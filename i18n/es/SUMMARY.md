# Spanish glossary and style sheet: what a reviewer should rule on first

No native reviewer has seen this draft. The files serve every Spanish system locale (`es-ES`, `es-MX`, `es-AR`, `es-419`…), so the draft aims at a neutral Spanish that reads naturally in Spain and in Latin America; a reviewer from either side should flag what reads as foreign to them. Each row gives the draft's choice (applied in `glossary.tsv` and `STYLE.md`) and the alternative. Most important first.

Microsoft publishes a Spain guide (MS-SG-ES) and a Mexico guide (MS-SG-MX); they are word for word the same on register, punctuation, error wording and key names, and differ only on regional vocabulary (ordenador/computadora, ratón/mouse, el PC/la PC, vídeo/video, coste/costo, leísmo, -se/-ra). Microsoft Terminology has a single Spanish set for every region.

Also open:

- **OS names.** Windows's names are the same in Spain and Mexico (MS-T; MS-Support es-es and es-mx): «Administrador de dispositivos», «Dispositivos de interfaz humana», «Otros dispositivos». 'USB Input Device' is **unverified** ('Dispositivo de entrada USB'): it needs a Spanish Windows. Apple's pages were approved for this run by the brief.
- **Electropedia** refused every fetch (403), so no IEV term backs any row.
- **Sources not reached:** Chauvin Arnoux's Spanish manuals (chauvin-arnoux.at serves a certificate for another host; not bypassed); no Spanish Extech or Steren manual on the makers' own sites; no Spanish UNI-T manual for the UT61E+ family. A manual from a maker in Spain would balance the Latin American leaning of Fluke's and Truper's Spanish.
- **Code gaps (not translation):** `MB`/`KiB`/`K` arrive preformatted (Spanish writes MB and KB too); `$keys` shortcuts come from egui in English.
